🪐 Why Planets Aren't Perfect Spheres: The Physics of Oblateness

1. Centrifugal Bulging & Hydrostatic Equilibrium

Self-gravitating fluid celestial bodies naturally relax into shapes governed by the balance between inwards gravity and outwards centrifugal force from rotation. The polar flattening is mathematically quantified by the ellipticity:

f = (R_equatorial - R_polar) / R_equatorial

A non-rotating body has f = 0 (perfect sphere). Rapid rotators like Saturn (f = 0.098) and Jupiter (f = 0.065) bulge massively at their equators, causing a visible squashed shape!

2. Saturn vs Jupiter vs Earth

  • Saturn: Flattening of 9.8%. Saturn's equatorial diameter is 120,536 km, but its polar diameter is only 108,728 km—a gap of 11,808 km (almost the diameter of Earth!).
  • Jupiter: Flattening of 6.5%. Even with immense mass, its 9.9-hour spin pushes the equator outwards by 9,276 km relative to the poles.
  • Earth: Flattening of 0.335% (21.3 km difference). This slight bulge makes Mount Chimborazo's peak farther from Earth's center than Mount Everest!
  • Haumea: Dwarf planet spinning once every 3.9 hours—so fast it deformed into a triaxial Jacobi ellipsoid (rugby ball shape) with a 2:1 length-to-height ratio!

3. Keplerian Elliptical Orbits & Kepler's 2nd Law

Planetary orbits are NOT circles! They are ellipses with the Sun located at one focus. In this simulator, each planet accelerates at perihelion (closest to Sun) and decelerates at aphelion (farthest from Sun) according to the exact Keplerian equation:
